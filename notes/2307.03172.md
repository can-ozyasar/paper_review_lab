> When AI language models are asked to answer questions using multiple documents, they actually perform worse than when they just rely on their own memory without any documents. In fact, models with larger memory windows often do no better than regular models at using the information given to them. 

Because AI struggles with this, we tested their basic search skills using a simpler test: finding a specific value inside a list of JSON data, just like looking up a phone number in a contact list. Even in this simple test, some AI models fail if the correct answer is hidden in the middle of the text. Their performance usually drops in the middle and is better at the beginning and the end, creating a U-shaped curve.

To understand why this happens, we tested different AI designs and training methods. We found that:
• "Encoder-decoder" AI models are better at finding information anywhere in the text, but only if the text is not longer than what they were trained to handle. If the text is too long, they also show the same U-shaped drop in the middle.
• Putting the question both before and after the text helps the AI pass the simple search test almost perfectly, but it does not help much with complex document questions.
• Even basic AI models—those that haven't been specially trained to follow instructions—still struggle to find information hidden in the middle of the text.
