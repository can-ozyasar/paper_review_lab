> Figure 1 shows that where you put important information inside a language model's input matters a lot. It creates a U-shaped curve. Models are good at using information if it is right at the beginning or right at the end, but they struggle a lot when the information is hidden in the middle. 

To handle tasks like searching through databases or long documents, language models need to process very long texts. Most current models use an architecture called Transformers, but as the text gets longer, they need a huge amount of memory and computer power. Because of this, older models were trained using relatively short text lengths, called context windows. Lately, better computer hardware and new algorithms have started to help fix this problem.
